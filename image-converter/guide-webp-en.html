What is WebP? Why You Should Switch from JPG & PNG

Notice more `.webp` files when downloading images from modern websites? Developed by Google, WebP has become the undisputed standard for web imagery. Here is everything you need to know about its compression mechanics, speed benefits, and how to convert your own images in seconds.

⚡ Quick Takeaway

WebP offers 25–35% smaller file sizes than comparable JPEG images at identical visual quality, and 26% smaller file sizes than PNGs while supporting full transparency and animation. For website speed, SEO, and storage savings, WebP is the clear winner.

1. What Exactly is the WebP Format?

WebP (pronounced "weppy") is an open-source raster image format introduced by Google in 2010. Its fundamental objective was straightforward: replace both aging JPEG (created in 1992) and PNG (created in 1996) formats with a single modern container engineered specifically for the high-speed web.

Unlike JPEG which only supports lossy compression and lacks transparency, or PNG which supports transparency but yields massive file sizes for complex photographs, WebP combines the strengths of both worlds into one versatile format.

3. The 3 Biggest Advantages of Switching to WebP

① Massive Bandwidth & Hosting Savings

Images account for over 60% of total page payload on an average website. Converting an e-commerce catalog or a photography blog from JPEG to WebP immediately cuts data transfer by 30% to 50%. This directly lowers cloud hosting bills (AWS S3, Cloudflare, Netlify) and accelerates data transfer for mobile users on cellular networks.

② Drastically Better Google Core Web Vitals (LCP Score)

Google explicitly prioritizes fast-loading websites in organic search rankings. The Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) metric measures how fast the main visual banner of your webpage renders. Because a WebP banner downloads twice as fast as a legacy JPG, switching to WebP is one of the quickest SEO upgrades you can deploy.

③ Lightweight Transparent Logos & Graphics

Graphic designers often struggle with heavy 24-bit PNG files for transparent headers, icons, and product cutouts. A transparent PNG that weighs 450 KB can easily be compressed into a crisp WebP file weighing just 65 KB — preserving crystal-sharp edges while eliminating 85% of unnecessary data weight.

4. Browser & Software Compatibility in 2026

In early years, WebP faced skepticism due to Apple's delayed adoption. However, since iOS 14 and macOS Big Sur, Safari has delivered full native WebP support. Today, every major browser renders WebP out of the box:

  • Google Chrome: Fully supported since version 32 (Desktop & Android).
  • Apple Safari: Fully supported on iOS 14+ and macOS 11+.
  • Mozilla Firefox: Fully supported since version 65.
  • Microsoft Edge: Fully supported across all Chromium builds.
  • Photo Editors: Adobe Photoshop 23.2+, Figma, Canva, GIMP, and Lightroom open and export WebP natively without extra plugins.

⚠️ When should you keep JPEG? If you are sharing images via legacy email clients, specialized enterprise software older than 10 years, or preparing high-resolution assets for professional physical printing (CMYK offset press), standard JPG or TIFF remains the safest choice.
